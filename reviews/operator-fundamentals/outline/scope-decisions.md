# Scope decisions and spec ambiguities

Phase 1 is the outline and the course entry on the site. It does not include lesson prose, quiz items, or a written final assessment.

## What was added

The owner specification lists 12 modules. It also requires the science behind each major system before the equipment. Those foundation topics are an added module, taught first: atoms, charge and electricity, magnetism and induction, pressure, temperature and heat, energy and work, fluid flow, and combustion. The 12 specified modules are still present and still cover every bullet in the specification.

The course therefore has 13 teaching modules and 46 lessons, plus a 30-question final assessment that is blueprinted and not written.

## Teaching order

The lessons are not in the specification's numeric order. The order follows what a beginner has to know before the next idea:

- Safety comes after the physical ideas and the picture of a plant, and before equipment walkdowns, rounds, and any talk of a start.
- Electrical fundamentals, including the generator, come before the gas-turbine lesson that says the shaft drives a generator.
- The facility survey comes after those principles, so each box on the map already has a meaning.
- Operating states come before the generic start-and-stop story.
- Instrumentation comes before duties that include reading screens, and before abnormal conditions.
- Markets come before duties that include a dispatch instruction.
- Terminology and the role-titled conversations come last.

## Lesson shape

The site's audited lesson is the shape used here: a 60-minute target, a hard cap of 90, short sections, checkpoints, and a knowledge check. The specification asks for at least five beginner questions. This outline plans 10, which is the site's standard and still satisfies that minimum. The items are not written in this phase.

Every lesson in the data file is 75 or 83 minutes. The longer ones add one core section. Both lengths are under the cap.

Each lesson record names the objectives, the example, the key terms, an SVG diagram, and a video candidate with a watch-for line and two takeaways. No video was selected or checked.

## What this course will not claim

It does not qualify anyone to operate, and it does not authorize hazardous work. Lockout, confined-space entry, and starting a unit are named so the learner recognizes them. The steps remain in the plant's procedures.

No setpoint, alarm limit, start duration, purge time, cooldown time, or valve lineup is stated. Where a design varies, the outline says so. Starter type is one of those variations, and it is not assigned to Viper Power.

Viper Power appears only as the owner described it: six GE Frame 7B simple-cycle peakers in ERCOT. Output, heat rate, and shaft speed are not given for that plant. The synchronous-speed arithmetic in the generator lesson is the physical relationship at 60 hertz. It is not a claim about that plant's rotor.

Heat rate, when the markets lesson is written, will use the public EIA explanation. Combined cycle is taught as a different design. ERCOT and PJM are labeled examples. A rule from one region is not treated as a rule in the other.

Example conversations use the role titles operator, supervisor, and dispatcher. They do not use personal names, and they do not contain a start command.

## Ambiguities

The specification's module 2 says not to go into detailed design. The first-principles section asks how each machine works. The outline puts the mechanism in the foundation lessons and in the gas-turbine and electrical modules, and uses the facility module for identification, interaction, and what the operator monitors.

Availability language is not the same in every market. The lessons define the words in operator language. If a later draft quotes a reliability glossary, that entry has to be opened first. The plant's own procedure still governs the local word.

The final assessment's 30 questions are split 5, 8, 7, 5, and 5 across industry, equipment, operating concepts, safety, and duties. That split is a planning choice so each required area is present. The items themselves are phase 2.

## What did not change

The project-manager course lessons, quizzes, and week routes are unchanged. The home page and the header now list both courses.
